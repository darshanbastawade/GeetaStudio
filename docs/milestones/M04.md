## Implemented behavior

CanonicalCorpus implements the framework-free ICanonicalCorpus application port. Domain rules, owned DTOs, six relational corpus tables, staff endpoints and a thin chapter reader preserve exact source text/hash independently from derived search normalization. Versioned manifests carry stable locators and explicit edition variants. Import validates Unicode, verse markers, SHA-256, source retention, exact snapshot membership, locale, duplicate locators, boundaries and immutable replay; transaction audit/command records preserve the result.

Source permissions and snapshot approval remain separate from corpus approval. Reader/report queries recheck current source rights and ownership; no global role substitutes for tenant membership. Pending/rejected/restricted states never count as approved readable material. Revocation impact now exposes corpus dependents. Concepts, interpretations and generated paraphrases are separate reviewed records, never substitutions for canonical quotation/translation.

Selected source: Annie Besant, Bhagavad-Gita, fourth edition (1922), Wikisource pinned transcription. Discourse 1 verses 1–25 are reproduced unchanged as decoded visible source text, with locator, UTF-8 checksum, raw HTML checksum, attribution and saved CC BY-SA 4.0 evidence. Sanskrit and English have independent source/permission records. Review evidence explicitly means automated transcription/hash/verse-locator checks; **no new human scholarly/manuscript/theological review is claimed**. Details and applicable material license: [fixture evidence](../fixtures/M04/README.md).

Manifest: 700 baseline plus Besant's chapter 13 opening Arjuna-question variant, 701 expected entries. Edition 13.2–13.35 map to baseline 13.1–13.34. Missing inventory remains metadata, never placeholder approved text. Transliteration, word meanings and approved explanations are absent and visibly reported; full generated commentary/multilingual content remain later work.
